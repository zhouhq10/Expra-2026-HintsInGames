### Categories and examples

#### 1. Spatial / navigation puzzles
Move an agent through a grid to a goal, with constraints.
- **Examples:** 
    - Sokoban 
        - Try some online: https://www.sokobanonline.com/
        - Example JS-based project: https://github.com/taniarascia/sokoban
    - Lights Out: https://www.logicgamesonline.com/lightsout/
    - Simple maze with keys/gates/switches
    - Rush Hour
- **Strength:** easy to hint ("push the box left first"), easy to log moves, clean state.
- **Watch out:** can be trivially brute-forced if too small.

#### 2. Rule-induction / pattern discovery
Player must infer a hidden rule from feedback.
- **Examples:** 
    - Mastermind
    - Zendo: does web versions exist?
    - Bongard problems
    - ARC 2 & 3: https://arcprize.org/arc-agi/2 & https://arcprize.org/arc-agi/3
- **Strength:** hints can target the *hypothesis* a player is considering - rich for strategy/reflective conditions.
- **Watch out:** individual differences in baseline ability are large; need enough trials.

#### 3. Sequential planning
Build a sequence of actions toward a goal.
- **Examples:** 
    - Tower of Hanoi
    - Missionaries & Cannibals
- **Strength:** very well-studied in cog-sci; existing models (means-ends, MCTS) available for week 9–10 modeling.
- **Watch out:** visually dry; motivation can be low.

#### 4. Constraint-satisfaction
Fill a structure subject to rules.
- **Examples:** 
    - small Sudoku (4×4 or 6×6)
    - Nonograms
- **Strength:** clean correctness metric; hint can point at a cell or a rule.
- **Watch out:** large knowledge-base differences across participants.

### Criteria a candidate game must satisfy

A task is only acceptable if it meets all of these:

- **Web-implementable** by HTML/JS or jsPsych.
  - *Bad:* anything needing Unity, a physics engine, or a native app.
- **Discrete, turn-based actions** - no real-time skill component or motor control.
  - *Bad:* Tetris, Angry Birds, platformers - reaction time and aim confound the hint effect.
- **Short trials** - solvable in 30 s to 3 min by an average adult.
  - *Bad:* The Witness or Baba Is You - a single puzzle can take 20+ minutes.
- **Multiple puzzle instances** - need ≥6 for main task + ≥2 for transfer, at similar difficulty.
  - *Bad:* narrative escape-room games - each puzzle is bespoke, no matched set.
- **Non-trivial but non-ceiling** - unaided solve rate should be relatively low, leaving room for hints to help.
  - *Bad:* tic-tac-toe (adults always draw → ceiling); full 9×9 Sudoku for novices (floor).
- **Attachable hints** - a well-defined "next move" or "key insight" exists per puzzle.
  - *Bad:* Wordle - the "right hint" is just "a better word," collapsing all 3 hint conditions into one. Minecraft-style sandboxes - no goal state, no principled hint.
- **Low prior-knowledge dependence** - a physics PhD shouldn't beat a novice by 10×.
  - *Bad:* crosswords (vocabulary), chess puzzles (club players dominate), trivia games.
- **Observable progress state** - from the action log, we can tell whether the participant is actively exploring or stuck/plateaued. Needed so hints can be triggered at the right moment and so "time to solve" is interpretable.
  - *Bad:* pure-thought puzzles with long silent pauses and one final answer (e.g., Einstein-style logic puzzles) - a 2-minute pause could be deep reasoning or total confusion, and we can't tell.

Nice-to-haves:
- Existing open-source web implementation to fork (check licenses).
